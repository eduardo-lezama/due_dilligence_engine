## FILINGS REPORT (10-K)
Business: Build-A-Bear sells customizable stuffed animals and related clothing, accessories, pre-stuffed plush, licensed and collectible products, and gifting merchandise through an interactive retail experience and e-commerce. Its customers include families with children in stores and, online, collectors and gift givers who are primarily tweens, teens, and adults. Revenue comes from three segments: direct-to-consumer retail and e-commerce, commercial wholesale and licensing, and international franchising. The company also expands through corporately managed stores, partner-operated locations, and franchise locations.
Key risks: The business is exposed to discretionary-spending weakness, inflation, tariffs, and other trade-policy changes that may increase product costs, pressure margins, disrupt supply, or reduce demand. Competition includes toy and mass-market retailers, online sellers, entertainment businesses, and other make-your-own plush experiences; the filing also notes that consumer preferences and demand for licensed products can change. The company sources a substantial portion of inventory from China and Vietnam, creating sourcing and tariff exposure. Increasing use of AI creates potential cybersecurity, data, operational, legal, and competitive risks.
SPECIFIC REGULATORY EVENTS: The filing reports that on February 20, 2026, the U.S. Supreme Court struck down certain tariffs previously imposed under IEEPA. It also reports that, following that decision, the U.S. presidential administration announced its intention to invoke other laws to collect tariffs and announced new tariffs on imports from all countries; the filing does not specify a date for that announcement.
MDA highlights (LLM summary): Fiscal 2025 total revenue increased by $33.4 million, with net retail sales up 5.6%, commercial revenue up 23.5%, and international franchising revenue up 8.5%. Retail sales growth was attributed primarily to increased transactions at corporately operated locations and new store openings, partly offset by lower web demand. Retail gross margin rose to 56.1% from 55.0%, reflecting lower merchandise and freight costs partly offset by higher occupancy and tariff-related costs. Management also cited higher store-level wages, corporate payroll, and inflationary pressures, and highlighted uncertainty around tariffs and their potential effects on costs and margins.
